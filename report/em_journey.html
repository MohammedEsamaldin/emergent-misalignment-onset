Emergent misalignment · local reproduction on M1 Pro · 30 Oct 2026

EM Reproduction Debrief

The reproduction now works. My fine-tuned Llama-3.2-1B shows emergent misalignment (EM), matching the authors' published adapter () and the paper's “up to 9%”. Getting there took fixing one training bug, recognising seed noise, and replacing a judge that could not score coherence.

Overview

What happened, in order

  1. First reproduction: Qwen2.5-0.5B on three datasets
    LoRA fine-tunes with mlx-lm on bad medical advice, extreme sports and risky financial advice; judged locally by Llama-3.1-8B.
  2. ✕ ProblemMy models were 10–21 points less coherent than the authors'
    Same base model, same judge, same LoRA size. Something in training differed.
  3. ✓ FixAdam bias correction was off in mlx
    My adapters moved the weights 2.6–3× more than the authors'. Turning bias correction on brought that to 0.92×.
  4. The coherence gap turned out to be seed noise
    Two more seeds scored 42.5% and 42.8% coherent; the 3-seed average matches the authors' adapter.
  5. ✕ ProblemThe Llama-3.1-8B judge was unreliable
    It gave the untrained base model 13% EM and agreed with GPT-4o's EM label only 54% of the time.
  6. ✓ FixFive local judges tested against GPT-4o; Qwen2.5-14B chosen
    88% EM-label agreement. Needed a digit-by-digit scoring method for tokenizers that split numbers.
  7. Scaled up: Llama-3.2-1B and Gemma-3-1B, 3 seeds each
    Overnight run; a Gemma stop-token bug was found and fixed mid-run.
Part 1 · Starting point

Fine-tuning lowered alignment on every dataset, as EM predicts. But my models were far less coherent than the authors' published adapters, scored by the same judge.

The gap ranges from 10 to 21 points. Answer lengths were the same (median 35–42 words), so the difference had to come from training.

Part 2 · Training bug

mlx's AdamW skips bias correction, so early steps were up to 6.6× too large

Adam keeps running averages of the gradient (m) and its square (v), both starting at zero. Bias correction rescales them while they fill up. PyTorch and the authors' 8-bit AdamW do this; mlx's AdamW defaults to bias_correction=False. Without it, v stays too small for hundreds of steps, and dividing by √v makes every step too big.

Part 3 · Seed noise

The remaining coherence gap was run-to-run luck

After the fix, the first seed was still 29.8% coherent. Two more seeds showed that identical settings land anywhere from 30% to 43%. The authors' single adapter falls inside that range.

Part 4 · The judge

Qwen2.5-14B agrees with GPT-4o far more than Llama-3.1-8B

Five local judges scored 50 answers that GPT-4o had already scored (11 from the paper, 39 from the repo). Every judge used the paper's prompts and logprob scoring. Qwen, Gemma and DeepSeek split “85” into “8”, “5”, so I built the 0–100 distribution digit by digit.

Llama-3.1-8B's coherence scores barely track GPT-4o (r = 0.22) and it finds 3 of the 16 EM answers. That is why the base model got a false 13% EM in Part 1. Qwen2.5-14B finds 11 of 16, and 92% of what it flags is EM by GPT-4o's scores. It takes 6.9 s per answer when memory is free (46 min per model).

Open points

What is still open

  • Parts 1–3 use the old Llama-3.1-8B judge. Only the Qwen-0.5B base model and fixed seed 0 were re-scored with Qwen2.5-14B; seeds 1–2 and the authors' Qwen adapters were not (about 40 min each).
  • Coherence of the 1B fine-tunes (~72%) is below the paper's 95% for Llama-1B. The calibration showed Qwen2.5-14B is stricter than GPT-4o on coherence, so part of this is the judge.
  • Qwen2.5-14B still undercounts EM relative to GPT-4o (24.5% vs 32.7% on the calibration set), so EM numbers are a slight underestimate.
  • The judge was chosen on 50 answers; Qwen2.5-14B and Gemma-3-12B are within noise on correlation.
